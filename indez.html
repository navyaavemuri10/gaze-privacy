Privacy Policy for Gaze

Last updated: REPLACE_WITH_DATE

The short version: Gaze does not collect, transmit, or sell any personal data. Everything it remembers stays on your own device.

What Gaze stores

Gaze saves a small amount of information locally on your computer using Chrome's storage, so it can remember your setup between sessions:

  • The number of breaks you've taken
  • The date you started using Gaze
  • Your preferences (idle detection, screen sharing detection, pause state)
  • Timer state, such as when your next break is due

This information never leaves your device. There is no Gaze account, no server, and no analytics.

What Gaze does not do

  • It does not collect personal or identifying information
  • It does not track your browsing history or the pages you visit
  • It does not read, store, or transmit page content
  • It does not send data to any external server
  • It does not use analytics, advertising, or third-party trackers
  • It does not sell or share data with anyone

Permissions and why they're needed

  • Storage — saves your break count and preferences on your device.
  • Alarms — schedules the 20-minute reminder.
  • Idle — detects when you step away so reminders pause instead of piling up. Gaze only reads whether the system is active, idle, or locked. It cannot see what you are doing.
  • Notifications — shows a reminder if the popup can't open on its own.
  • Scripting and website access (optional) — used only if you turn on "Screen Sharing Detection." When enabled, Gaze checks whether a screen share has started so it can pause reminders and avoid interrupting your presentation. It looks only at whether sharing is active — never at page content. This permission is off by default and you can revoke it at any time by turning the setting off.

Children's privacy

Gaze does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13, because it does not collect information at all.
